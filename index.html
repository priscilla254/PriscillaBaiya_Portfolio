About

I'm set to graduate in November 2026 with a Distinction in MSc Computer Science (Artificial Intelligence), and I'm looking for a graduate role where I can keep doing what I enjoy most: taking a messy, real problem and using data to build something that solves it.

During my placement as a data scientist at Costplan, a quantity surveying consultancy, the company's cost data was unstructured, inconsistent, and scattered across years of individual spreadsheets. I standardised the inputs, then built a pipeline to validate and load that data into a SQL Server warehouse. The proof of concept showed the different ways centralised data could actually be used, from a guarded natural-language layer for querying the data directly, to Power BI-ready views for benchmarking, to automated report drafting. I presented the proposal directly to the company's directors, and it's now signed off as a two-phase project.

Alongside that, my MSc research explored synthetic data for facial age estimation. I built a generative pipeline and evaluated how fairly it performed across different demographic groups, then extended it by asking a simpler question: could better starting images fix some of the bias I'd found? I tested different image-generation models to see whether the base identities they produced made a difference.

That same instinct, starting from something unclear and working through it to a solution that holds up, is what drove my most recent personal project: a RAG assistant that answers construction and site-safety questions, citing the exact regulation or guidance paragraph behind each answer rather than letting the model guess.

Outside of work, I'm usually training for something, whether that's hiking, running, or the gym. Otherwise I'm probably a few chapters into a thriller.
